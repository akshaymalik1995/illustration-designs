Scarlet Block Cutout Doodle Book Cover - Design Language

A stark literary cover system built from a white paper field, oversized scarlet serif letterforms, one central photoreal cutout object, rough black marker contour drawing, small bookish serif typography, and strong asymmetrical negative space.

Apply this visual language to whatever subject the user asks for - any medium, any composition. Where a trait below mentions posters, typography, or campaign framing, treat it as optional flavor, not a requirement; the palette, texture, lighting, and mood are the style.

- Overall Style Category: conceptual literary book cover photographic cutout over typography scarlet and black editorial minimalism rough marker intervention poster absurd object-as-character cover
- Composition Logic: The design starts as a spare white book-cover field. Huge scarlet red serif or slab typeforms act as architecture, filling the center and lower frame while being cropped at the edges. A realistic cutout subject sits on top of those letters, mostly vertical, interrupting the red shapes. A rough black hand-drawn contour line is added as a disruptive second skin around the subject. Small serif text is used as furniture, not as a dense text block.
- Subject Placement: The subject should occupy the central vertical axis or a slightly off-center position and overlap the biggest red letters. It should feel pasted onto the cover, not photographed in a scene. The black marker contour can extend around the subject into one or two exaggerated loops.
- Camera And Perspective: Use flatbed, catalog, or object-cutout photography with minimal perspective. The object is front-facing or slightly three-quarter, isolated cleanly from the white ground. Avoid deep environments.
- Typographic Character: Use crisp old-style or slab serif type in uppercase. The red typeforms are not meant to be fully readable; they are monumental cropped graphic blocks. Smaller text can be legible but invented, with a vertical title strip on one side and a top-left author-like line.
- Texture And Finish: White paper grain, print-like red ink, clean photographic object detail, and an uneven black marker line. The line should feel physically drawn over a printed cover.
- Mood: Dry wit, literary absurdity, museum-bookshop design, tactile print, spare but confrontational.
