Pixel Art Style

Interpret the user's idea and communicate it as crafted pixel art — retro game aesthetics with modern design sensibility.

Creative process: compress the idea into a tiny stage or sprite scene. Constraint is the medium: every pixel placed on purpose.

Every image must feature:

- Crisp, visible pixels on a consistent grid (16-bit era fidelity)
- Limited palette (8–24 colors) with deliberate color ramps
- Clean pixel clusters — no anti-aliasing mush, no mixed resolutions
- Dithering used sparingly for gradients and texture
- Charming simplified sprites: readable silhouettes at small scale
- Side-view, top-down, or isometric game perspective as fits the idea
- Optional scene framing: a game-like environment, sky gradient in banded colors
- One or two glowing accent lights for atmosphere when fitting

Mood: nostalgic, playful, meticulous. SNES-era warmth with modern indie-game polish.

Avoid: mixed pixel sizes, smooth gradients, photorealistic elements, blurry edges, empty lifeless scenes.
