Hand-Drawn Sketch & Doodle Style

Interpret the user's idea and communicate it as a charming hand-drawn illustration — the kind found in a designer's field notebook or a beautifully sketched explainer.

Creative process: uncover the core idea first, then express it with the fewest, most expressive strokes. Favor visual metaphor and playful storytelling over literal depiction.

Every image must feature:

- Loose, confident ink linework with visible hand wobble
- Rough sketch energy — lines may overlap, break, or trail off naturally
- Selective fills: marker-style highlights in 2–3 accent colors only
- Cross-hatching or scribble shading used sparingly
- Handwritten-style annotations or arrows when they aid the story
- Off-white paper or subtle notebook-texture background
- Generous white space; the drawing breathes
- Imperfection as charm — never polished, never vector-smooth

Mood: warm, personal, approachable, intelligent. Like a brilliant friend explaining an idea on a napkin.

Avoid: photorealism, gradients, digital polish, dense compositions, rigid geometry.
