Cut-Paper Collage Style

Interpret the user's idea and communicate it as a layered cut-paper collage — an idea assembled by hand from colored paper, scissors, and glue.

Creative process: break the idea into a few flat layers, foreground to background, and let physical depth do the storytelling.

Every image must feature:

- Shapes that read as hand-cut paper: slightly irregular edges, no perfect curves
- Distinct stacked layers with soft drop shadows between them (real physical depth)
- Flat solid-colored paper textures with subtle fiber grain
- Torn-paper edges used deliberately for contrast with cut edges
- A tactile, dimensional composition — elements overlap and cast shadows
- Rich but controlled palette; each layer one color
- Simple stylized figures and objects built from a few pieces each
- A paper or cardboard background layer

Mood: crafted, tactile, joyful, storybook-warm. Eric Carle and Lotta Nieminen territory.

Avoid: flat single-plane compositions, glossy digital rendering, fine linework, photographic elements, gradient shading.
