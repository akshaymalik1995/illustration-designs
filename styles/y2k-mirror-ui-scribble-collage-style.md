Y2K Mirror UI Scribble Collage - Design Language

A gritty Y2K photomontage style that combines wide-angle flash photography, floating desktop-like interface fragments, thick electric-blue marker outlines, hand-drawn graffiti lettering, and dense analog noise into a chaotic personal-screen collage.

Apply this visual language to whatever subject the user asks for - any medium, any composition. Where a trait below mentions posters, typography, or campaign framing, treat it as optional flavor, not a requirement; the palette, texture, lighting, and mood are the style.

- Composition Logic: A primary flash-photo snapshot fills the page, then multiple secondary image panels and fake interface cards are layered on top as if captured from a messy personal computer screen. The hierarchy is intentionally overloaded: a large subject, one loud scribble headline, one blue drawn contour, and several small UI interruptions.
- Subject Placement: The subject is cropped close with wide-angle distortion, often leaning toward the viewer or crossing the lower and center zones. Secondary cutouts sit on one side or in corners, never as clean balanced modules.
- Camera Perspective: Use fisheye or phone wide-angle perspective with one object or body part exaggerated in the foreground, hard flash, reflective surfaces, and casual off-axis framing.
- Surface Texture: The entire artwork should feel printed, rescanned, and compressed: salt-and-pepper noise, sharp halos, grainy shadows, and imperfect cutout seams.
- Ui Layering: Generic desktop-like and mobile-like UI fragments float above the photo: tiny window chrome, simple folders, alert cards, verify panels, and small image thumbnails, all simplified and unbranded.
