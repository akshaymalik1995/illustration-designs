Bold Block Mascot Poster - Design Language

A dense flat poster system built from giant black display type, chunky white mascot figures, thick cartoon outlines, tilted cyan or mint color panels, compact badges, tiny red accents, and a clean off-white print surface.

Apply this visual language to whatever subject the user asks for - any medium, any composition. Where a trait below mentions posters, typography, or campaign framing, treat it as optional flavor, not a requirement; the palette, texture, lighting, and mood are the style.

- Style Category: flat editorial mascot poster, comic-sticker poster system, bold portfolio cover graphic
- Composition Logic: The image is built from flat layers: off-white paper, giant black type, a backing panel, one diagonal color slab, a simplified foreground mascot or subject, then one small accent mark. The typography works as architecture rather than captioning.
- Subject Placement: The main subject is large and cropped near the lower edge or lower corner. It overlaps the diagonal panel or headline and reads as a sticker-like graphic object instead of a detailed illustration.
- Camera Perspective: Orthographic flat poster perspective with graphic diagonal movement. Avoid natural camera depth, realistic space, or cinematic lighting.
- Typography Behavior: Ultra-black display lettering uses huge scale, tight spacing, stacked words, vertical title blocks, cropped edges, and compact side labels. Text is short and graphic.
- Color Behavior: The base is off-white and deep black. Cyan, mint, lemon, teal, or related bright panels form the major graphic mass. Red is a small editorial punctuation mark or card accent.
- Graphic Elements: Speech bubbles, badges, stamps, prohibition symbols, thick borders, portfolio cards, tab strips, side indexes, flat grids, and microtype blocks create density.
- Texture Finish: Flat raster illustration with vector-like hard fills, subtle paper grain, compressed screenprint edges, and thick uniform linework.
- Mood: Playful, blunt, design-culture adjacent, editorial, portfolio-ready, slightly sarcastic, and poster-like rather than narrative or realistic.
