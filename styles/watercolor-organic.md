Watercolor & Organic Style

Interpret the user's idea and communicate it as a soft, luminous watercolor illustration — emotion carried by pigment and water.

Creative process: find the feeling inside the idea first; let color temperature and flow express it. Composition stays simple so the medium can breathe.

Every image must feature:

- Translucent watercolor washes with visible blooms and soft bleeding edges
- Layered transparency — colors mixing where washes overlap
- White of the paper preserved as light; unpainted areas are part of the design
- Loose, gestural shapes; edges dissolve rather than end
- Subtle granulation and paper texture
- A restrained, harmonious palette (2–4 hue families)
- Optional fine ink or pencil linework beneath the washes, sparingly
- Airy, uncluttered composition

Mood: warm, tender, human, contemplative. Children's-book gentleness with editorial sophistication.

Avoid: hard vector edges, digital gradients, saturated neon color, dense detail, dark heavy backgrounds.
