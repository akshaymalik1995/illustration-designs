Coral Window Megatype Motion Poster - Design Language

A tactile editorial motion-poster system built from a coral-red paper field, one cool pale-blue photographic window, colossal black condensed typography that crosses the image boundary, a single isolated action subject, sparse micro-editorial labels, and a bold geometric direction symbol.

Apply this visual language to whatever subject the user asks for - any medium, any composition. Where a trait below mentions posters, typography, or campaign framing, treat it as optional flavor, not a requirement; the palette, texture, lighting, and mood are the style.

- Composition Logic: A large coral paper field holds one cool photo rectangle. Oversized black words cross the rectangle and outer field in opposing orientations, while one cutout action figure ties the flat type and photographic depth together.
- Subject Placement: The subject is large, nearly full-body, and positioned around the central third so limbs or a prop break at least one photo-window boundary and interrupt the headline.
- Camera Perspective: Neutral to slightly low editorial camera with a clean open background, enough distance to read the body gesture, and no extreme lens distortion.
- Typography System: Ultra-heavy condensed neo-grotesk capitals function as slabs of black mass. One word can rotate vertically; other words stack or extend off-canvas. Microcopy is tiny, narrow, and rigorously aligned to the margins.
- Color Behavior: Coral red is the dominant graphic field, powder blue fills the photo window, and near-black typography supplies maximum contrast. Skin, fabric, and props remain warm-muted with at most one quiet accent.
- Graphic Elements: Use one bold block-built arrow or chevron, short hairline rules only when needed, and tiny issue-like labels. Avoid stickers, bursts, ornamental frames, and UI chrome.
- Texture And Lighting: Soft directional daylight, lightly subdued highlights, subtle film grain, uncoated paper tooth, mild ink spread, and tactile screen-printed black areas.
- Mood: Kinetic yet disciplined editorial design: experimental fashion energy contained by a rational modernist grid.
