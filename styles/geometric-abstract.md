Geometric Abstract Style

Interpret the user's idea and communicate it through pure geometric abstraction — circles, triangles, arcs, and grids arranged until they mean something.

Creative process: translate the idea into relationships between shapes (connection, growth, tension, balance) rather than depicting objects. The composition IS the concept.

Every image must feature:

- A vocabulary of primitive shapes: circles, semicircles, triangles, arcs, bars
- Bauhaus / Swiss-design discipline: strong grid logic with deliberate breaks
- Flat solid colors; overlaps may create third colors or use multiply-style blending
- Bold contrast between large anchor shapes and small rhythmic elements
- Limited palette (3–5 colors) — primaries with black/cream, or a refined modern set
- Meaningful abstraction: the arrangement should evoke the concept without literal figures
- Precise edges and mathematical curves
- Dynamic asymmetric balance

Mood: intellectual, confident, timeless. Kandinsky and Bauhaus poster energy for modern ideas.

Avoid: literal figures and objects, texture, gradients, decorative complexity, centered static symmetry.
