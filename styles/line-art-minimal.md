Minimal Line Art Style

Interpret the user's idea and communicate it as refined single-line or monoline art — maximum meaning from minimum ink.

Creative process: reduce the idea to one essential gesture or silhouette. If it cannot be drawn in a few continuous strokes, simplify the concept further before drawing.

Every image must feature:

- Uniform monoline strokes (single consistent weight throughout)
- Continuous, flowing lines — elegant curves, deliberate simplicity
- One accent color at most, applied as a single flat shape or blob behind the linework
- Vast negative space; the subject occupies a fraction of the canvas
- Minimal or abstract faces (a dot, a curve, or nothing)
- Plain background: white, off-white, or a single muted tone
- Compositional balance that feels intentional, like a logo mark

Mood: sophisticated, calm, gallery-worthy. Matisse's cut-line economy meets modern brand identity.

Avoid: varying stroke weights, shading, texture, fills beyond the single accent, busy scenes, more than one focal subject.
